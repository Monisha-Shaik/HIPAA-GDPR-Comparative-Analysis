# Proposed Regulatory Changes

This register identifies selected proposed changes relevant to health data privacy and security under HIPAA and GDPR. Proposed provisions are distinguished from requirements already applicable. The research cutoff is October 31, 2026.

## PC1 Proposed HIPAA Security Rule Changes

**Title:** HIPAA Security Rule to Strengthen the Cybersecurity of Electronic Protected Health Information.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**Publication date:** January 6, 2025.

**Official proposal URL:**\
[https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-01-06/pdf/2024-30983.pdf](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-01-06/pdf/2024-30983.pdf)

**HHS explanation URL:**\
[https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/index.html](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/index.html)

**Legal status:** Proposed rule. The 2026 regulatory agenda lists this action under Long-Term Actions, with final action projected for July 2027.

**Status source URL:**\
[https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=202510&RIN=0945-AA22](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=202510\&RIN=0945-AA22)

**Relevance:** This proposal helps the study examine how HIPAA may respond to cybersecurity risks in digital healthcare. It connects the research’s focus on electronic health information with proposed changes to organizations’ responsibilities for identifying risks, implementing safeguards, and demonstrating accountability.

**Proposed changes overview:** The proposal seeks to strengthen and clarify security requirements for covered entities and business associates. The study will examine how these proposed duties differ from the existing Security Rule and what they could mean for healthcare organizations. The current Security Rule remains applicable during rulemaking.

## PC2 Proposed HIPAA Privacy Rule Changes

**Title:** Proposed Modifications to the HIPAA Privacy Rule to Support, and Remove Barriers to, Coordinated Care and Individual Engagement.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**Publication date:** January 21, 2021.

**Official proposal URL:**\
[https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2021-01-21/pdf/2020-27157.pdf](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2021-01-21/pdf/2020-27157.pdf)

**HHS explanation URL:**\
[https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/hipaa-care-coordination/index.html](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/hipaa-care-coordination/index.html)

**Legal status:** The 2026 regulatory agenda places the associated rulemaking at the Final Rule Stage. This describes an agency planning stage and does not, by itself, establish that a final rule has been published or become applicable.

**Status source URL:**\
[https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=202510&RIN=0945-AA00](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=202510\&RIN=0945-AA00)

**Relevance:** This proposal helps the study examine how HIPAA seeks to balance privacy protection with access to health information and coordinated care. It connects the research’s focus on privacy obligations and individual rights with proposed changes affecting information sharing between healthcare organizations, individuals, and caregivers.

**Proposed changes overview:** The proposal addresses individuals’ access to their health information, information sharing for care coordination, family and caregiver involvement, and disclosures in emergencies. The study will examine the proposed balance between facilitating healthcare and maintaining privacy protections.

## PC3 Proposed GDPR Digital Omnibus Changes

**Title:** Digital Omnibus proposal concerning the simplification of the digital legislative framework, including proposed GDPR amendments.

**Publisher:** European Commission. The Council of the European Union provides subsequent negotiating materials.

**Publication date:** November 19, 2025, for the Commission proposal.

**Commission announcement URL:**\
[https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/simpler-digital-rules-help-eu-businesses-grow-2025-11-19_en](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/simpler-digital-rules-help-eu-businesses-grow-2025-11-19_en)

**Council negotiating position URL:**\
[https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/10/21/digital-rulebook-council-agrees-position-to-simplify-data-protection-privacy-and-cyber-reporting-rules/](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/10/21/digital-rulebook-council-agrees-position-to-simplify-data-protection-privacy-and-cyber-reporting-rules/)

**Legal status:** The Commission proposed amendments, and the Council agreed its negotiating position on October 21, 2026. The Council announcement was updated on October 22 with its mandate. These negotiating materials do not establish enacted GDPR amendments.

**Relevance:** These materials help the study examine how proposed GDPR reforms could affect health information protection and organizational responsibilities. They connect the research’s focus on changing requirements with proposals concerning sensitive data, scientific research, and breach reporting.

**Proposed changes overview:** The study will examine the GDPR-related provisions separately from other legislation included in the Digital Omnibus package. It will distinguish the Commission’s original proposals from the Council’s later negotiating position, particularly where their approaches differ.
