# HIPAA Source Register

## HO1 HIPAA Regulations

**Title:** 45 CFR Part 160 General Administrative Requirements and 45 CFR Part 164 Security and Privacy.

**Publisher:** Office of the Federal Register, National Archives and Records Administration, and U.S. Government Publishing Office, through the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (eCFR). The regulations are issued by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).

**URLs:**

- [45 CFR Part 160 General Administrative Requirements](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-160)
- [45 CFR Part 164 Security and Privacy](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164)

**Relevance:** These regulations provide the primary legal foundation for examining how HIPAA protects health information. They allow the study to establish what HIPAA requires directly from the regulatory text, giving the research a reliable basis for its analysis and findings.

## HO2 Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule

**Title:** Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**URL:** [Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html)

**Relevance:** This HHS summary helps the study understand HIPAA’s approach to protecting the privacy of health information. It explains the relationship between protecting individuals’ information and allowing its use for healthcare purposes, supporting the research’s examination of privacy obligations and individual rights.

## HO5 Business Associates Guidance

**Title:** Business Associates Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs).

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**URL:** [Business Associates Frequently Asked Questions](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)

**Relevance:** This HHS guidance helps the study examine how responsibility for protecting health information extends beyond healthcare organizations to external service providers. It connects the research’s focus on digital healthcare and third-party involvement with the obligations of organizations that handle health information on another organization’s behalf. It supports understanding how agreements and direct responsibilities help maintain privacy and security when information passes between organizations.
