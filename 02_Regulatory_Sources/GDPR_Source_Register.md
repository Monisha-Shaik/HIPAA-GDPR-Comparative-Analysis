# GDPR Source Register

This register identifies core sources for the GDPR analysis. GO1 and GO8 are legislation; the other entries are official explanatory or interpretive materials. The comparison matrix supplies additional topic-specific authorities. The research baseline is October 31, 2026; later application dates are identified separately.

## GO1 GDPR Regulation

**Title:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 General Data Protection Regulation.

**Publisher:** Official Journal of the European Union, available through EUR-Lex. Adopted by the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union.

**URL:** [https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02016R0679-20160504](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02016R0679-20160504)

**Relevance:** This regulation provides the primary legal foundation for examining how GDPR protects personal data, including health information. It allows the study to establish GDPR’s requirements directly from the legal text and understand how health data protection fits within its broader approach to individual rights and organizational responsibility. It provides a reliable basis for the research’s analysis and findings.

**Source type and version:** EU legislation, consolidated text identified by CELEX 02016R0679-20160504. Consolidated texts aid reading; the authentic Official Journal acts and corrigenda remain the legal sources. The version identifier is not the research cutoff.

## GO2 Official GDPR Overview

**Title:** The general data protection regulation.

**Publisher:** Council of the European Union, through its official Consilium website.

**URL:** [https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/data-protection-regulation/](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/data-protection-regulation/)

**Relevance:** This official overview helps the study understand GDPR’s overall approach to protecting personal data. It connects the research’s focus on privacy and security with the relationship between individuals’ rights, organizational responsibilities, and regulatory oversight. It provides context for interpreting the legal text in GO1 and explaining GDPR’s approach in the research paper.

**Source type:** Official overview, used for context rather than as a substitute for the regulation.

## GO3 Legal Grounds for Processing Personal Data

**Title:** Legal grounds for processing data.

**Publisher:** European Commission.

**URL:** [https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en)

**Relevance:** This page supports the study's examination of lawful processing, consent and alternatives to consent. It explains legal grounds for personal-data processing generally; it is not dedicated health-data guidance. For health data, the analysis must also identify an applicable Article 9 condition alongside the Article 6 basis, using GO1. This distinction helps the research avoid treating consent as the only route or a general legal basis as sufficient on its own.

## GO4 Organizational Obligations

**Title:** Obligations.

**Publisher:** European Commission.

**URL:** [https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/obligations_en](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/obligations_en)

**Relevance:** This overview supports the research's examination of organizational accountability, information duties, impact assessments, data protection officers and breach response. Its coverage is broader than security and risk assessment. It provides an entry point for identifying relevant duties; the matrix grounds specific comparisons in the corresponding GDPR articles and detailed guidance.

## GO5 Controller and Processor Guidance

**Title:** Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of controller and processor in the GDPR.

**Publisher:** European Data Protection Board (EDPB).

**URL:** [https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-072020-on-the-concepts-of-controller-and-processor-in-the-gdpr_en](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-072020-on-the-concepts-of-controller-and-processor-in-the-gdpr_en)

**PDF:** [Final guidance Version 2.1](EDPB_Guidelines_07_2020_Controller_Processor_Final.pdf)

**Relevance:** This EDPB guidance helps the study understand how responsibility for protecting personal data is allocated between organizations. It connects the research’s focus on digital healthcare and external service providers with the distinction between organizations that decide how and why data is processed and those that process it on another organization’s behalf. It supports examination of accountability when health information moves between healthcare organizations and their service providers.

**Source type and version:** EDPB interpretive guidance, adopted July 7, 2021, with minor corrections in Version 2.1 dated September 20, 2022. The linked repository PDF is retained as a supporting document; the guidance interprets controller and processor concepts rather than amending the GDPR.

## GO6 Personal Data Breach Notification Guidance

**Title:** Guidelines 9/2022 on personal data breach notification under GDPR.

**Publisher:** European Data Protection Board (EDPB).

**URL:** [https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-92022-on-personal-data-breach-notification-under-gdpr_en](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-92022-on-personal-data-breach-notification-under-gdpr_en)

**Relevance:** This EDPB guidance helps the study understand how organizations respond when personal data is compromised. It connects the research’s focus on health information protection with the assessment of harm to individuals and decisions about notifying authorities and affected people. It supports examination of organizational responsibility after a breach.

**Source type and version:** EDPB interpretive guidance, final Version 2.0, adopted March 28, 2023 and listed on the EDPB page April 4, 2023. Read with GDPR Articles 33–34.

## GO7 Enforcement and Penalties Guidance

**Title:** Enforcement and sanctions.

**Publisher:** European Commission.

**URL:** [https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/enforcement-and-sanctions_en](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/enforcement-and-sanctions_en)

**Relevance:** This European Commission guidance helps the study understand how GDPR obligations are enforced and how organizations are held accountable for noncompliance. It connects the research’s examination of privacy and security responsibilities with regulatory investigations, corrective measures, penalties, and individuals’ access to remedies. It provides context for explaining the practical consequences of failing to protect health information.

**Source type:** Official explanatory guidance. Statutory powers, remedies and fine criteria are established in the GDPR; this page provides context for interpreting them.

## GO8 GDPR Enforcement Procedural Regulation

**Title:** Regulation (EU) 2025/2518 laying down additional procedural rules on the enforcement of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

**Publisher:** Official Journal of the European Union, available through EUR-Lex. Adopted by the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union.

**URL:** [https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2518/oj/eng](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2518/oj/eng)

**Relevance:** This regulation helps the study examine how GDPR enforcement is developing when cases involve organizations and individuals in different EU countries. It connects the research’s focus on regulatory developments with efforts to improve cooperation between authorities and the handling of complaints. It supports understanding how enforcement procedures can influence accountability in cross-border healthcare environments. The regulation is in force, but its procedures apply from April 2, 2027, so the study will treat it as an adopted development with future application rather than procedures already applicable at the October 31, 2026 cutoff.

**Application and transition:** Article 37 sets application from April 2, 2027; Article 36 specifies which investigations and dispute-resolution referrals fall under the new procedures. Entry into force is distinct from application.
