# HIPAA Source Register

This register identifies core regulatory sources for the HIPAA analysis. The comparison matrix provides additional topic-specific authorities and pinpoint provisions. Regulations, official guidance and court-affected provisions are distinguished; the research baseline is October 31, 2026.

## HO1 HIPAA Regulations

**Title:** 45 CFR Part 160 General Administrative Requirements and 45 CFR Part 164 Security and Privacy.

**Publisher:** Office of the Federal Register, National Archives and Records Administration, and U.S. Government Publishing Office, through the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (eCFR). The regulations are issued by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).

**URLs:**

- [https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-160](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-160)
- [https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164)

**Relevance:** These regulations provide the primary legal foundation for examining how HIPAA protects health information. They allow the study to establish what HIPAA requires directly from the regulatory text, giving the research a reliable basis for its analysis and findings.

**Source type and version:** Binding regulations. The eCFR URLs above display current content and can change after the study cutoff. The official annual edition below is dated October 1, 2025 and is not, by itself, confirmation of the complete October 31, 2026 text. Court decisions and subsequent amendments must be considered separately. User-supplied eCFR text captures of Parts 160 and 164 display the version in effect on October 31, 2026 and were used to check the historical baseline. Court-affected provisions are interpreted alongside the cited HHS and judicial materials.

## HO2 Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule

**Title:** Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**URL:** [https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html)

**Relevance:** This HHS summary helps the study understand HIPAA’s approach to protecting the privacy of health information. It explains the relationship between protecting individuals’ information and allowing its use for healthcare purposes, supporting the research’s examination of privacy obligations and individual rights.

**Source type:** Official explanatory summary, read with the regulations in HO1. The November 2026 Privacy Rule finalisation is identified separately under PC2; it does not replace the October 31 baseline.

## HO3 Summary of the HIPAA Security Rule

**Title:** Summary of the HIPAA Security Rule.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**URL:** [https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html)

**Relevance:** This HHS summary helps the study understand how HIPAA addresses the security risks associated with electronic health information. It connects the research’s focus on digital healthcare with organizations’ responsibilities to assess risks and protect information through appropriate safeguards.

**Source type:** Official explanatory summary of the existing Security Rule. Proposed changes are identified separately under PC1.

## HO4 Breach Notification Rule

**Title:** Breach Notification Rule.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**URL:** [https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html)

**Relevance:** This HHS source helps the study examine how HIPAA responds when the privacy or security of health information has been compromised. It supports the research’s examination of how organizations assess breaches, communicate with those affected, and remain accountable after an incident.

**Source type:** Official summary of 45 CFR §§ 164.400–414. Use the regulatory provisions for precise notification triggers, recipients, timing and exceptions.

## HO5 Business Associates Guidance

**Title:** Business Associates.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**URL:** [https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)

**Relevance:** This HHS guidance helps the study examine how responsibility for protecting health information extends beyond healthcare organizations to external service providers. It connects the research’s focus on digital healthcare and third-party involvement with the obligations of organizations that handle health information on another organization’s behalf. It supports understanding how agreements and direct responsibilities help maintain privacy and security when information passes between organizations.

**Source type:** Official guidance on business-associate roles, agreements and direct liability. Legal definitions and duties are established by the regulations, not the page title alone.

## HO6 Enforcement and Compliance

**Title:** HIPAA Enforcement.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**URL:** [https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/index.html](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/index.html)

**Relevance:** This HHS source helps the study examine how HIPAA requirements are enforced in practice. It connects the research’s focus on organizational accountability with the investigation of complaints, corrective actions, and consequences of noncompliance. Its links to enforcement data, case examples, and resolution agreements can help the study explore practical challenges healthcare organizations face in protecting health information.

**Source type:** Official enforcement overview. Individual enforcement decisions illustrate application; they do not alone establish how frequently violations occur or how effective enforcement is.

## HO7 Reproductive Health Rule Status

**Title:** HIPAA and Reproductive Health.

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

**URL:** [https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/reproductive-health/index.html](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/reproductive-health/index.html)

**Relevance:** This HHS source helps the study examine how regulatory developments and court decisions affect health information privacy protections. It connects the research’s focus on current and changing requirements with the need to distinguish provisions that remain applicable from those set aside by a court. It helps prevent the study from presenting the 2024 reproductive-health privacy rule as fully operative.

**Legal-status context:** HHS reports that the June 18, 2025 order in Purl v. HHS vacated most of the 2024 reproductive-health rule. Specified notice provisions at § 164.520(b)(1)(ii)(F)–(H) were vacated; the remaining notice modifications were undisturbed, with compliance required by February 16, 2026. The page also retains earlier descriptions of the rule, which must be read subject to that status notice. The matrix lists supporting court documents separately.

**Subsequent appeal history:** The Fifth Circuit dismissed the proposed intervenors' appeal on September 10, 2025, on their motion. The government stated in its September 2 letter that it had not noticed an appeal. These hosted court and government documents support the procedural history; the matrix identifies them under HIPAA-36.
