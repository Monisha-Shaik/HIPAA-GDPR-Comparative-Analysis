# Limitations

This study uses comparative regulatory document analysis. It examines legal requirements and their organizational implications rather than how those requirements operate in practice. It does not measure compliance, implementation costs, security effectiveness, breach reduction, enforcement effectiveness or patient outcomes. Shared governance activities identified in the analysis should therefore be understood as opportunities for coordination, not demonstrated improvements in performance.

The legal scope is limited to federal HIPAA and EU-level GDPR requirements. US state law, Member State law, 42 CFR Part 2, the European Health Data Space and parallel reporting regimes—including national laws implementing NIS2 and the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule—are considered only where necessary to qualify an interpretation. They are not comprehensively compared. GDPR Article 9(4), for example, permits Member States to introduce further conditions concerning health data, including limitations. Consequently, the EU-level comparison does not establish all requirements applicable to a particular healthcare organization, research activity or data flow.

The findings reflect the legal baseline on October 31, 2026. Subsequent developments, including the November 2026 HIPAA Privacy final rule and the European Parliament’s Omnibus IV vote, may affect their practical relevance. Post-cutoff developments are distinguished from the historical baseline. Interpretations of pending proposals are conditional on the versions examined; the requirements ultimately adopted, their application dates and transitional arrangements may differ.

The analysis relied on English-language legal texts and accessible official materials, which may limit consideration of national interpretations and untranslated sources. Agency guidance informed interpretation but was not treated as binding legislation. Historical versions were assessed using dated captures and supporting official records; those materials remain subject to limitations in provenance and completeness. Court-affected provisions required separate examination of judgments and procedural status because the continued appearance of a provision in a regulatory text does not, by itself, establish its enforceability. These checks do not establish an exhaustive account of every potentially relevant legal development.

The comparison matrix and eight domains existed before the coding guide was formalized. The subsequent audit refined the analysis iteratively rather than constituting a prospectively specified independent coding exercise. The interpretations have not undergone independent legal review, which limits assurance that another reviewer would reach the same conclusions or identify the same exceptions. Topic counts describe the organization of the comparison; they are not independent observations and do not support statistical estimates of similarity, convergence or comparative effectiveness.

The literature review draws on a selected set of studies rather than an exhaustive systematic search. Its coverage limits the contribution claim: the study offers a source-linked comparison across the selected domains and cutoff, but does not establish that no equivalent comparison exists. Additional searching and specialist review may identify overlapping work or interpretations that require the analysis to be narrowed or revised.
