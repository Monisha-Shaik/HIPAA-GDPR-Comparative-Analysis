# Conclusion

This comparative document analysis examined 46 topics across eight domains of health-data privacy and security under HIPAA and GDPR, using an October 31, 2026 baseline. HIPAA assigns responsibilities through defined covered-entity and business-associate relationships, while GDPR assigns responsibilities according to processing roles and activities within its scope. Shared protection objectives coexist with differences in coverage, individual rights, safeguards, third-party obligations, notification, remedies and sanctions.

The central organizational implication is to distinguish shared governance activities from the legal decisions they support. Organizations may coordinate risk assessments, vendor records, incident investigations and supporting documentation, while separately determining each framework’s applicability, conditions and required actions. Cross-border processing particularly requires distinguishing whether GDPR applies, whether a disclosure constitutes an international transfer and which transfer route is available to the recipient.

The study contributes a source-linked account of these distinctions while separating applicable requirements, pending proposals and adopted measures with deferred application. If adopted in the examined form, selected reforms would alter specific duties—notably HIPAA encryption, authentication and recovery requirements, and GDPR authority-notification thresholds and timing—without making the frameworks interchangeable. The study does not rank the frameworks or establish their effectiveness in practice. Independent review and evaluation in representative healthcare settings are needed before the comparison can be presented as a validated operational decision-support instrument.
